# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

android

## Users

Pet tutors managing a dog's day-to-day care and health. Private, non-commercial household use — not a public or commercial product.

## Product Purpose

Log a dog's daily routines (walks, meals, medication) and health events (weight, vaccinations, deworming, incidents) with minimal friction, so no tracking data is lost during his critical early months. Success is real, sustained use — an entry logged after the walk or the vet visit, not an app that looks complete but goes unused.

## Positioning

**The name is the product thesis.** "Petfile" reads as the animal's file — a dossier or CV: the accumulated record of walks, vaccines, weights and incidents, not just a logger for today. Copy should nod to that where it has room. The Spanish word that carries the same double meaning is _diario_, which is at once the daily act of logging and the notebook the history accumulates in, which is why the login screen reads "El diario de tu compi". Avoid _expediente_ and _historial_: both are accurate and both sound like a vet's clipboard, against the household voice.

Built on a relational data model from day one, so that adding a second tutor or a second pet later is additive — a new row, never a schema redesign.

## Operating Context

Logging is mostly retrospective, not real-time during the event itself: a walk or a vet visit occupies the tutor's hands and attention, so the normal pattern is to log it after getting home, not mid-walk or mid-consultation. Concrete examples: logging a walk on returning home (whether the dog pooped and its state, any incident, an optional note) — a future version could even auto-detect "arrived home" via reconnecting to the home wifi; after a vet visit, logging the deworming/vaccine applied, letting the next due date calculate itself, updating weight, and keeping notes from the conversation with the vet (e.g. diet — kibble vs. natural food, pros and cons — or a possible spay/neuter date). Google sign-in requires a native development build; Expo Go cannot complete the OAuth redirect. A web export exists as a secondary target, not the primary one.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- **The day's log ships as one table, weights and treatments as their own** (resolved 2026-09-10). Walks, meals, medication and incidents are the same kind of fact — something happened at a time, read back as a list — so they share `pet_events`, with their specifics in `details`. Weights and vaccine/deworming records get real columns because the app _computes_ from them: a line and a due date. The rule the split follows, and the one to apply to anything added later: **what the app computes from gets columns; what it only lists does not.** A walk's minutes are the one promoted key, because the exercise goal is measured against their sum. An incident is a day-log row despite being health-shaped, for the same reason: nothing is derived from it yet.
- **A treatment's next date is stored, not computed** (resolved 2026-09-10). The vet says "in three months" and that instruction is the fact. The form proposes a date from a per-kind default so the tutor rarely types one — which is what "the next date calculates itself" means in practice — but the row keeps what they confirmed rather than a schedule the app invented.
- v0 (current): single user, Google OAuth only — no email/password UI, no development-mode auth bypass in any environment. Daily routines and health events both ship from day one (not sequenced). Weight is tracked as a simple line, without the breed-percentile comparison band. Due-date alerts (vaccines, deworming) surface inside the app only — no push notifications yet.
- Data model already supports v1 (sharing via `pet_owners`) and v2 (multiple pets/species) without restructuring; the UI for either doesn't exist yet.
- **Onboarding field hierarchy (resolved 2026-09-07, replacing the earlier "fast-follow?" question).** The pet form has three tiers, and the distinction is about what blocks a save, not about what is worth collecting:
  - _Required_ — name, sex, birth date (with its approximate flag). Validation blocks submission. The birth date is required deliberately: it is not a per-interest field like the ones below but a basic input that many later flows depend on from the start, and it anchors the vaccine and deworming due-date calculations.
  - _Required_ also includes sex, for now. Making it optional was considered on 2026-09-08 — it is arguably the same category as breed, an identification datum the product computes nothing from — but `pets.sex` is `not null` in the initial schema, so relaxing it in the app alone produces a save the database rejects. Left required deliberately; revisit with the migration, not before.
  - _Identity, optional_ — breed (with its mixed-breed flag). Does not block submission, but sits with the required fields rather than below them: name, breed and age (derived from the birth date) are what let a tutor tell one animal from another once more than one exists.
  - _Optional, deferrable_ — sterilisation, activity level. Kept in the data model because they feed weight tracking and future nutritional guidance, not dropped. The onboarding form offers a secondary "fill in more details" disclosure for them, but the intended path is to ask each one when a flow that depends on it starts (sterilisation at a health or nutrition flow, not at signup).
- **An approximate birth date means month + year only** (resolved 2026-09-07). Stored as `YYYY-MM-01` with `birth_date_approximate = true`, which the initial schema already supports — no migration needed. Any consumer that computes a due date must read that flag: the day is a placeholder, not data, and treating it as real reintroduces exactly the invented-date problem the flag exists to prevent.
- **Dates are entered and displayed in the Spanish locale format `DD/MM/AAAA`** (resolved 2026-09-07); a per-user format setting is a future version, not v0. The wire format stays ISO `YYYY-MM-DD` because the `birth_date` column is a Postgres `date` and the RPC casts with `::date`, where a non-ISO string is ambiguous. Conversion is two pure functions at the edge in `lib/`, not a translation layer between the app and Supabase.
- **Four columns in `pets` are shipped-schema-with-a-purpose, not leftovers** (resolved 2026-09-10). They existed from the initial schema with no code referencing them, which reads as scaffolding; each one has a surface waiting for it:
  - `photo_url` — the animal's avatar on the profile, and the thumbnail that tells one pet from another once there is more than one. It is also the single thing that makes the record feel like _his_ file rather than a form.
  - `exercise_goal_minutes` — the daily walk/exercise target the day view tracks against. It is what turns a list of logged walks into progress.
  - `vet_primary` / `vet_emergency` — contact cards on the health screen: name, phone, location. Stored as `jsonb` because a card is a small shape that will grow (a clinic's hours, a second phone) and none of it is queried.
- **Adding an entry lives in a floating action, near the thumb** (resolved 2026-09-10). The four kinds were four buttons across the top of the day view — one tap, at the far end of the screen from the hand holding it, and occupying the space the log itself should have. The floating action costs a second tap and buys back both. The nearest option to the thumb is the walk, because it is the likeliest.
- **Every entry is a correction waiting to happen** (resolved 2026-09-10). Tapping a row reopens the sheet it was created from, prefilled, so fixing the grams of food or the time of a walk is the same form that recorded it. Deleting is inside that sheet and never in the list: a destructive control in a scrolling list is a mis-tap waiting for a scroll. It asks once, in place.
- **Navigating between days, and the calendar as a record** (resolved 2026-09-10, shaped first). Arrows either side of the day's heading, no future beyond today, and a calendar that drops from the top when the date is tapped. Two different jobs were on the table — "did he walk yesterday?" and "which day did he have diarrhoea?" — and only the first is served by arrows: nobody walks three weeks back a day at a time. So **the calendar carries the weight, which means it has to say what happened on each day**; a calendar with no marks is a date picker, and it answers only the question you already know the answer to.
- **The marks reserve an _alert_ colour for what is rare** (resolved 2026-09-10, amended the same day on the device). About one incident and one medication a month against eleven days short of the goal: the common states are a month's background texture and differ in _weight_, the rare ones are events and carry the only hue on the screen. Incident is Error Red, which **extends that colour's meaning** — it used to be "the app failed or something is about to be destroyed", and an incident is something going wrong with the _animal_. That is the honest extension: a tutor scanning a month for trouble should find red. Medication is Warning Amber because these were framed as **alerts**, days that needed attention; as a mere category ("this day has medication") amber would be overloading a status colour. **And the goal is not an alert either, but it is not rare** — about eighteen days of a month meet it, so whatever colour that mark wears becomes the calendar's background rather than its exception. It gets the quiet half of the accent family, Aqua Glaciar, and the day view's bar was repainted to match: a light neutral while it fills, the secondary accent when it is met. Success Green went back to meaning a **moment** — a toast, the confetti — rather than a state that sits on the screen all evening.
- **One vocabulary across the two surfaces** (resolved 2026-09-10). The calendar spoke in dots and rings and the log in glyphs and words, and nothing tied a red dot on the 9th to the row that put it there. A 6px mark cannot carry a glyph, so **colour is the channel**: the two rare kinds wear their calendar colour in the log, the goal mark wears the day bar's own colour, and the same state is called the same thing in the legend, in the log and out loud — "Objetivo conseguido".
- **No mark is ever dropped because another one outranks it** (resolved 2026-09-10). Medication and an incident on the same day used to collapse into the red dot alone — which threw the medication away on the one day it mattered most, the day something also went wrong. Each kind now has its own corner of the cell. The rule generalises: a summary may compress, but it may not silently discard, and a rare combination is the case a record exists for.
- **Meals carry no mark, and the reason is a modelling statement** (resolved 2026-09-10): eating is assumed, so **the absence of a meal is not data — it is an incident somebody has to log.**
- **The exercise goal is not versioned, and that is accepted** (resolved 2026-09-10). `pets.exercise_goal_minutes` is a single current value, so a "met the goal" mark on a day three weeks back is judged against today's target: raise the goal and every past day retroactively falls short. The mark answers "would this day meet today's target", which is still a trend worth seeing, and the calendar's legend names the threshold so the rule is never invisible. Storing a goal per day is a schema change for a v0 nicety.
- **An entry can be logged into a past day** (resolved 2026-09-10). A diary whose forgotten Tuesday can never be filled in is a diary that punishes the forgetting. The sheet names the day when it is not today; nothing in the validation changed, because every time check already compared an instant against the real now.
- **Finding an entry by its words is a separate tool** (2026-09-10). Searching for "diarrea" is a full-screen result list, not a calendar, and bundling it here would double this feature and leave the marks half-finished. The marks cover the case they can — a month at a glance — and text search is the next shape.
- **A walk is logged by its two ends, and the duration is calculated** (resolved 2026-09-10). DESDE and HASTA are what a tutor actually knows — they remember leaving and getting back, not how many minutes passed — so asking for the duration was handing them the app's arithmetic. The duration stays editable because "unos cuarenta minutos" is a real memory of a walk, and it counts **backwards from the end** — which is corrected from the first version, where "now" prefilled the start. Logging is retrospective: the tutor is standing there having just got home, so the time they are sure of is the one the walk finished at, and that is the field that must never shift under them. It also has **-15 / +15**, because the fastest walk to log is the one with nothing to say about it — tap Paseo, tap +15 three times, save. The three other kinds are still one moment, because they are.
- **Durations are read as hours past the hour** (resolved 2026-09-10). "45 min" under an hour, "1h 30m" over it, "5h" when the minutes are zero — everywhere the app shows a length of time, and accepted in both forms wherever it takes one. "90 min" is a number the reader has to divide; a walk and a daily target are both things a tutor thinks about in hours. Bare minutes keep working, so nobody loses the habit they already have.
- **The diary reads forwards** (resolved 2026-09-10). Earliest entry at the top. Newest-first suits a feed someone dips into; this is one day, short, and read whole.
- **The avatar is a circle, and the app frames the photo itself** (resolved 2026-09-10, reversing the square). Tapping the portrait opens a sheet that shows the photo inside the circle it will appear in, with drag to position and pinch or buttons to zoom. The system cropper is off: it could only offer a square preview of a round result, and cropping twice throws away the pixels framing needs. What is stored is the crop, so reframing later means picking the photo again — the sheet says so rather than enlarging 512 pixels into a frame that wants more.
- **The profile presents the file and puts the form behind a door** (resolved 2026-09-10). The screen a tutor opens to look at their dog reads as a record — photo, name, breed, age — not as eight inputs waiting to be corrected, and each block carries its own "Editar…" button. Two consequences the product wanted anyway: the four fields the header presents are the ones worth filling even though only two of them block a save, so the header says out loud what is missing and offers the door; and deleting the record is no longer reachable from a screen someone opened to look at their dog.
- **A dog's age carries its life stage** (resolved 2026-09-10). The header reads "2 años · Adulto" — cachorro under 6 months, adolescente to 18, adulto to 7 years, senior after that. It is the first piece of advice the app can give away from data it already holds, and it is deliberately one curve for every dog: body size moves the senior threshold by years, and `breed_primary` is free text with no weight band behind it. Refine it when breed carries a size, not by guessing.
- **What gets deleted is the file, not the animal, and the wording says so** (resolved 2026-09-10). "Borrar la ficha de Loki", never "Borrar a Loki". One of the reasons a tutor reaches that button is that the animal has died, and a confirmation written about the dog asks them to agree to a sentence about their dog rather than about a record in an app. The ceremony stays — the name has to be typed back, case-insensitively — but the name is asked for in the field's label rather than shown as an example beside it: the label is uppercased by the type scale, so "ESCRIBE LOKI" over a field holding "Loki" read like a mismatch the tutor had to fix.
- **Duplicate-pet-name warning is a v2 requirement, not a v0 one** (resolved 2026-09-07). It should be a non-blocking warning that helps a tutor spot an accidental duplicate, but v0 is single-pet — onboarding redirects away once a pet exists and `getMyPet` returns one row per account — so there is no code path that could trigger it yet. It belongs with the multi-pet form, alongside the undecided invitation mechanism.
- **Resolved additions to the visual system** (2026-09-07): checkbox, breed combobox with autocomplete, and an in-app date picker join the component set, and the app gains its first iconography. These are recorded in DESIGN.md as they are built, not before — DESIGN.md describes what ships. Two constraints came out of the decision: a checkbox or check glyph must not be a Unicode character (the Outfit typeface's charset does not cover them, so they silently fall back to another family, which the existing sex-chip glyphs already do), and the date picker is built in-house rather than delegated to the platform, because `@expo/ui`'s DateTimePicker renders nothing at all on the web target used for development and has no month-and-year mode.
- Explicitly undecided: the tutor-invitation mechanism for v1 (6-digit code vs. direct link); cosmetic app identity (icon/splash colors, license file) still carries Expo's scaffold defaults.
- Android is the platform the app actually ships to and is used on — both household members install and run it there. The browser is the development and QA harness (most visual work is judged there first), and a web export exists as a secondary target, but the delivered product is an Android app. iOS is buildable from the same React Native codebase but is not a target for anyone in the household. The visual system stays uniform regardless (see Brand Commitments).

## Brand Commitments

**Celebration.** Registering the pet is worth marking with a confetti effect — approved 2026-09-08. It earns the exception because v0 is single-pet: onboarding runs _once in an account's life_, so the effect can never become the repeated noise that usually ruins it. Two conditions: it must be disableable exactly like every other animation in the app (the system "remove animations" setting, via `useReducedMotion()`), and it must be hosted above the navigator like the toast, or the success navigation will unmount it before it lands.

**Failure is part of the voice.** The app takes responsibility rather than reporting a stack: two sentences cover every failed request — the connection dropped, or the fault is ours — and the transport's own message goes to the console. A tutor logging a vet visit on a weak signal is the normal case, not the edge one.

**Voice.** The app speaks like a household, not like enterprise software. Prefer warm, specific wording over neutral system verbs — a primary action reads "¡Vamos Loki!" or "Añadir mascota", never "Guardar" or "Enviar" — and use the animal's own name wherever it is known, which is also the only reliable way a tutor sees their input read back. Playful phrasing drawn from the product's world (pets, walks, vet visits, training) is welcome where it does not obscure what a control does; the moment a joke makes an action ambiguous, the action wins. Where a primary action and its confirmation belong together they are written as a call and its answer — "¡Vamos, Loki!" then "¡Loki ya está contigo!" — which is a recall, drawn from how the tutors actually talk to the animal. Three places the voice does **not** go: field labels and other structural text, which must read instantly; errors, which must be unambiguous before they are warm; and anything naming an internal plan rather than what the tutor sees. Copy stays in Spanish.

**The voice is for the eye, and the accessible name is for the ear.** Warm copy works on screen because the form around it supplies the context; read out on its own, "¡Vamos, Loki!" is an exclamation and not a control. So every playful label carries a literal accessible name — the button announces "Registrar mascota" — and emoji never reach one, since a screen reader says "cara de perro" where a tutor sees 🐶. This is not a dilution of the voice: it is what lets the voice be as warm as it likes without costing anyone the action.

"Nordic Ice" visual identity — a dark-mode-only palette and the Outfit typeface — is a fixed constraint the user chose to carry into this project and preserve, kept as the one visual constraint while everything else was decided fresh. Token values are implemented in `global.css`. It governs every platform as one uniform system: adapt it to each platform's physical constraints (safe areas, native gesture zones) rather than swapping in platform-native components (SF Symbols, Material 3 widgets) to satisfy per-OS conformance. Android is the primary adaptation target — the physical constraints that govern layout decisions are Android's (status and system navigation bars, gesture insets, back-gesture edges, the range of Android screen densities). Web is the development surface and a secondary export, not the platform the design is tuned for.

## Product Principles

- Ship something usable today over a complete feature set; the MVP evolves with real use, not ahead of it.
- Never lose tracking data — the app's core promise during Loki's critical early months.
- Grow the data model, not rebuild it: sharing and multi-pet support must stay additive.
- Low-friction capture beats completeness — logging happens after the fact (back home, after the vet), so it should be quick to fill in retrospectively, not built to survive real-time, one-handed use.
- Be honest about gaps in the product and its docs — no implied capability (a dev bypass, CI/CD, automation) that doesn't exist yet.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific accessibility need identified for either household member (confirmed with the user). General good practice is already built into the design tokens: WCAG AA contrast on the Nordic Ice palette, no state communicated by color alone, and standard mobile touch-target sizing.
